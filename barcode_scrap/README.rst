Scrap damaged or lost goods directly from the Barcode scanner app.

The operator picks the internal location the goods are written off from,
scans them (with a GS1 parser installed, a GS1 label also gives its
lot/serial and piece count), sets the quantities, chooses the scrap
reasons and scraps. Each line becomes a native ``stock.scrap``, created
and validated on the spot, so the stock leaves the location for the
scrap location exactly as it does from the back office.

This is a feature module on top of ``barcode_scanner``: it registers its
own screens, scan handling and home-screen tile into the scanner core
without patching it, so you install only what you need.

Usage
=====

From the Barcode app home screen, tap **Scrap Products**.

Scrap goods
-----------

1. **Pick or scan the location** the goods are written off from.
   Scanning a location barcode jumps straight to the scrap lines;
   otherwise choose one from the searchable list of internal locations.
2. **Scan each product** to scrap: a plain barcode adds one unit. With a
   GS1 parser installed, a GS1 label also gives its **lot/serial** and
   its **piece count** (AI 30/37); a net weight on the label is not
   taken as the quantity. Scanning the same product and lot again adds
   to its line. Tap **"+"** to add a product by hand; with no search it
   lists what the location holds. Only goods can be scrapped, as in the
   back office: services are not offered.
3. Each line shows how much of it the location holds. Adjust the
   quantity if needed.
4. Choose the **scrap reasons** and tap **Scrap**. A reason that does
   not exist yet can be created on the spot with **New reason**, as the
   back office's reason field allows; typing the name of an existing one
   (in any case) picks that one instead of creating a duplicate.

Lots and serial numbers
-----------------------

A lot is found by its exact name: lots whose names differ only in case
are different lots. A name typed in another case finds its lot only when
it names a single one.

A lot/serial-tracked product needs its lot/serial: read from the GS1
label, or typed into the line, which suggests the lots stored in that
location. When the location holds a single lot of the product, it is
filled in. A serial number is scrapped one unit at a time and can only
appear once.

Stock shown and not enough stock
--------------------------------

The quantity each line shows is what a scrap made from the scanner can
take: the net quantity at the location itself, outside packages and not
owned by a partner, as validating the scrap counts it. Goods in a
package or owned by a partner are scrapped from the back office, where
the scrap takes a package and an owner.

When a line asks for more than that, nothing is scrapped yet: the
scanner lists what is short and asks whether to **scrap anyway** — the
same choice the back office offers through its insufficient-quantity
wizard. Confirming scraps the lines it was asked about; lines changed in
between are checked again.

Destination
-----------

The goods go to the scrap location the back office preselects for the
company, and can be sent to any scrap location of the company or a
shared one instead. When the company has none of its own, the first
shared one is taken; with no scrap location at all, scrapping waits
until one is created.

Reading more from a label
-------------------------

The quantity a scan stands for comes from
``ScrapScreen.scannedQuantity(scan, productUomId)``: the piece count, or
one unit. A module that reads more from a label (a GS1 net weight, say)
can patch it to convert that into the product's unit, as
``barcode_stock`` offers with its own ``scannedQuantity``.
